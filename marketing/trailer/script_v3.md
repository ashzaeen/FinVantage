# FinVantage MavPitch Phase I video - Production Script v3

Working title: "One Tower"
Recurring metaphor: your money is ONE tower of Jenga blocks. Each goal is a block (Trip = teal, Laptop = slate grey, Emergency fund = coral, Rent = mustard, the same four colours everywhere). A purchase is pulling a block. Most apps tell you after the tower falls; the Copilot speaks up before you pull.

Rubric tags: R1 Overview/Problem, R2 Opportunity/Solution, R3 Product/Competitive Advantages, R4 Offering, R5 Marketing and Sales/Market, R6 Competitive Landscape, R7 Revenue Model, R8 Current Status/Go To Market, R9 Communication.

Narration convention: every NARRATOR line is read by one human-sounding AI voice, and is written exactly as the voice model should say it (numbers spelled out, "Projection Lab" as two words). On-screen text uses numerals. The founders speak only in their own segment.

What changed from v2 (answering every consensus fix)
1. The business half of the video is now 70 s (1:45 to 2:55), up from 49 s in v2 (revenue alone goes from 8 s to 23 s), and every number in it is locked: free dashboard, Copilot and Advisor paid at $99 a year after a free trial, the 10.7% industry median, 16,000 to 48,000 PAYING users, $1.6M to $4.8M a year, about $72,000 a year per 1,000 paying users, and the exact $4,000 / $3,000 / $8,000 split. (Fixes 2, 3, 4.)
2. Revenue (2:12 to 2:35) is a spoken funnel: what is free, what is paid, the price, the industry-median conversion, the paying-user math, the contribution line. On screen: the full funnel (150,000 to 450,000 installs at 10.7% = 16,000 to 48,000 paying users x $99), why not pure freemium (2.1% would need 0.8M to 2.3M installs), rival price anchors, and "assumes $18/yr bank data cost, unverified". (Fix 3.)
3. Competition (1:45 to 2:00) gives each rival a fair strength in plain words (Monarch, Rocket Money with Rowan AI, Projection Lab and Boldin simulate the future), concedes the planners, then one line on why it is hard to copy. Cleo and Copilot Money are on screen with a strength and a gap each. (Fix 6.)
4. "So the Copilot sees everything you own" is gone. Replaced by the privacy line at 1:00 ("only four fields leave the page; your goals and balances stay in your account") and a 6 s shared-pool animation at 1:17 ("every goal is funded from the same money, so one purchase can move all of them"). (Fix 1.)
5. Go-to-market (2:00 to 2:12) states one customer path, said once: UTA students through a low-cost campus pilot, then 25 to 34 year old young professionals via the Chrome Web Store and creators, later bank and credit union partners. The 34% app-use stat is on screen only. (Fixes 2, 8.)
6. Status: "No users yet" is said once (0:52, by a founder on camera), followed by the founders' own commitment: 10 pilot testers by the Dec 3 finale, 100 by spring. The ask (2:35 to 2:53) closes with what the pilot measures and "then we apply for production bank access". "92 commits" is cut from voice and screen; 315 tests stays on screen. Each founder states a role in one clause. (Fixes 4, 8.)
7. Advisor (1:34 to 1:45) opens with what it is, says each question once and matches the screen, and the trip question is "How likely is my Japan trip?" so the answer is a range, not advice. TD trust stat moved on screen. (Fix 5.)
8. The opening stat is now card debt ($1.29 trillion, NY Fed) tied to the gap: "your app tells you next month". The stake "$3,299 laptop vs your Japan trip" is on screen from 0:00. Silent opening and the 6 s silence are cut (one 3 s chime beat remains, as the reveal). (Fixes 7, 9.)
9. Pacing: every beat is at or under 2.8 spoken words per second, measured by script (table in section (a)). Decorative lines cut: "Every purchase feels like one small choice", "Built by four of us", the spoken list of seven account types, the duplicate trust lines.
10. Production fixes: G1 cut (the opening is now the real checkout recording); G2 hand tagged @Maya; G3 is stop-motion only (no slow-motion conflict); G4 simplified (no crowd) and moved to a physics-strong model with its no-credit fallback set as the default if the first draft fails.
11. New section: "Likely judge questions and 1-line answers".
Kept from v2: the Jenga rewind gag, the laptop vs Japan trip card, the dashboard beat (8 s plus 4 s reused under "Free" = 12 s of dashboard footage), real screen recordings only for the product, few generated clips (3), the schedule, the self-check.

## Likely judge questions and 1-line answers
1. Why would anyone pay $99 when Monarch or Copilot Money already exist? They tell you what you spent; we tell you, at checkout, what this purchase does to every goal at once, and the dashboard that pulls your accounts together stays free, so trying us costs nothing.
2. Is 16,000 to 48,000 paying users realistic? It is 0.1% to 0.3% of our estimated 16M reachable users and needs about 150,000 to 450,000 installs at the 10.7% industry median for trial apps; that is why we chose a paid plan behind a free trial rather than freemium, which at the 2.1% median would need 0.8M to 2.3M installs.
3. Is this financial advice, and is the data safe? No: it gives educational projections, only suggests, never moves money, sends four fields from the page, and code computes every number; the $4,000 GLBA Safeguards readiness review comes before any real account, and we apply for production bank access only after it.
4. What actually works today? A working prototype built in 2 days at HackUTD (2nd place, PNC track) with 315 tests passing on Nov 8, running on sandbox data; the Copilot works on our demo sites and Amazon product pages; there are no users or revenue yet, and we have committed to 10 pilot testers by Dec 3 and 100 by spring 2027.
5. Why start with students, and where does this go long term? Students are our first, low-cost test group at a 42,787-student campus, and the pilot measures weekly use and how many would pay $99 a year; then 25 to 34 year old professionals, where 34% already use budgeting apps, and later bank and credit union partners, where our PNC track result is a natural bridge; Phase II (up to $25,000 more) is the next funding step.
