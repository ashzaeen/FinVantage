# FinVantage MavPitch Phase I video - Production Script v4

Working title: "One Tower"
Recurring metaphor: your money is ONE tower of Jenga blocks. Each goal is a block (Trip = teal, Laptop = slate grey, Emergency fund = coral, Rent = mustard, the same four colours everywhere). A purchase is pulling a block. Most apps tell you after the tower falls; the Copilot speaks up before you pull. In the business half the same blocks become the funnel and the ask.

Rubric tags: R1 Overview/Problem, R2 Opportunity/Solution, R3 Product/Competitive Advantages, R4 Offering, R5 Marketing and Sales/Market, R6 Competitive Landscape, R7 Revenue Model, R8 Current Status/Go To Market, R9 Communication.

Narration convention: every NARRATOR line is read by one human-sounding AI voice, and is written exactly as the voice model should say it (numbers spelled out, "Projection Lab" as two words). On-screen text uses numerals. The founders speak only in their own segment.

What changed from v3 (one line per numbered fix)
1. Free-tier cost (fix 1): spoken at 2:02 "The dashboard is free with one linked account, so free users cost little", and at 2:07 the free trial is what unlocks every account. "After costs" is now "after variable costs" (2:30). On screen: blended margin is set by the free-to-paid mix, which the pilot tracks.
2. Conversion wording (fix 2): "ten point seven percent of people who start the free trial pay" (2:13). The 2.1% freemium downside gets its own card and a spoken line (2:26): "At freemium rates, it takes about five times the installs."
3. Competitor line (fix 3): "none steps in at checkout" and "hard to copy" are gone. Now: "None of them checks a purchase against all your goals. Ours does, with a shared-pool simulator and math the AI can't fudge, both tested." (1:34 to 1:51).
4. Pilot milestone (fix 4): "by December third" (no finale). Testers are named as 10 UTA classmates and friends; the target is 6 of 10 weekly use and 3 of 10 who say they would pay $99, said aloud as a target (2:43). The path is spoken: the campus pilot measures what each install costs, then the Chrome Web Store and creators (1:51 to 2:02). 315 tests passing is the big caption at the high-five (0:49).
5. O17 split (fix 5): the funnel card now carries three numbers only (trial starts, 10.7%, paying users giving $1.6M to $4.8M), drawn as tower blocks; the freemium downside is the next card; market sizing moved to the roadmap card.
6. Hero payoff spoken (fix 6): "A card says this laptop drops your Japan trip from on track to unlikely, and suggests waiting or something cheaper." The card callouts lead with "Wait" and "Cheaper option"; the 0% promo option, if the real card shows it, is labelled "if you already have one".
7. Problem in student terms (fix 7): 0:14 now says "Most apps show what you spent, not what it costs your goals." $1.29 trillion stays on screen with its NY Fed tag.
8. Jargon (fix 8): "code-checked numbers" is now "math the AI can't fudge"; "four fields" is now "only four things: price, store, item type and page type". The close is "Security review first, then real banks. Know before you buy." spoken over the standing tower.
9. Runtime (fix 9): 418 down to 393 spoken words, numerals as spoken. Every beat is at or under 2.6 words per second (measured, section (a)); the busiest number beat runs at 2.54.
10. Founders (fix 10): roles moved to lower-thirds. The 30 s is now the gag plus one true sentence from Aram on why they built it ([TRUE STORY], with a fallback line), then proof, "No users yet", and the commitment.
11. Accuracy (fix 11): O12 says "12 demo accounts" with no type count; the "2nd place, PNC track" label is removed from the bank-partner step and appears only in the founders segment, tagged "competition result".
Also fixed: the dashboard beat no longer stamps all 12 accounts as "FREE" (that would contradict the one-account free tier); it now reads "Free: 1 linked account. Free trial and paid plan: all accounts."
Kept from v3: funnel math, the exact $15,000 split (on screen, amounts spoken), students-first path, "No users yet" said once by a founder, Rocket Money's Rowan AI, the shared-pool animation, 3 generated clips with fallbacks, the judge Q&A, the Jenga rewind gag, real screen recordings only for the product.

## Likely judge questions and 1-line answers
1. Why would anyone pay $99 when Monarch or Copilot Money already exist? They show what you spent; we check, at checkout, what this purchase does to every goal at once, and the dashboard stays free with one linked account, so trying us costs nothing.
2. What do free users cost you? Little: a free user links one bank account, so the bank data cost is capped at one account, and linking every account comes only with the free trial and the paid plan. The about $72 is per paying user after variable costs and before fixed costs, and it assumes an unverified $18 a year bank data cost; the blended margin with free users depends on the free-to-paid mix, which the pilot tracks, and real data costs are measured once real accounts launch after the security review.
3. Where does 10.7% come from, and what if you convert worse? It is the RevenueCat 2026 industry median (115,000+ apps, Day 35) for apps with a paid plan behind a free trial, not our own number, and we describe it as the share of people who start the trial who pay. We have not measured our own conversion; the sandbox pilot measures weekly use and how many say they would pay (target 3 of 10). If we only reach the 2.1% freemium median, the same 16,000 to 48,000 paying users need 0.8M to 2.3M installs, about five times as many, which is why the paid plan sits behind a trial instead of a pure freemium model.
4. Is 16,000 to 48,000 paying users realistic? It is 0.1% to 0.3% of our estimated 16M reachable users over 3 years and needs about 150,000 to 450,000 trial starts at the industry median; published market sizes differ about 100x, so we built ours bottom-up.
5. Your first 10 testers are classmates and friends. Isn't that biased? Yes, and we say so: it is a first check of weekly use and stated willingness to pay, with a bar set in advance (6 of 10 weekly, 3 of 10 would pay $99); the 100 testers by spring come through the low-cost campus pilot, which is also where we measure cost per install.
6. What does a customer cost to acquire? We do not have a number yet and will not guess one; the campus pilot measures cost per install before we spend on the Chrome Web Store and creators.
7. Is this financial advice, and is the data safe? No: it gives educational projections, only suggests, never moves money, sends only four things from the page, and code computes every number; the $4,000 GLBA Safeguards readiness review comes before any real account, and we apply for production bank access only after it.
8. What actually works today? A working prototype built in 2 days at HackUTD (2nd place, PNC track, a competition result) with 315 tests passing on Nov 8, running on sandbox data; the Copilot works on our demo sites and Amazon product pages; there are no users or revenue yet, and we have committed to 10 testers by Dec 3 and 100 by spring 2027.
9. Where does this go long term? Consumer subscriptions first (UTA students, then 25 to 34 year old professionals, where 34% already use budgeting apps), then bank and credit union partners; Phase II (up to $25,000 more) is the next funding step.
