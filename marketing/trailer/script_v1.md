# FinVantage MavPitch Phase I video - Production Script v1

Working title: "One Tower"
Recurring metaphor: your money is ONE tower of Jenga blocks. Each goal (Trip, Laptop, Emergency fund, Rent) is a block. A purchase is pulling a block. Other apps photograph the rubble after it falls; FinVantage's Copilot is the hand that stops you one second before you pull. The tower is the same physical object in the trailer, the founders segment (real lawn Jenga) and the explainer (3D paper-craft animation), and it reappears inside the Copilot card and the simulator.

Rubric tags used below: R1 Overview/Problem, R2 Opportunity/Solution, R3 Product/Competitive Advantages, R4 Offering/Traction, R5 Marketing and Sales/Market, R6 Competitive Landscape, R7 Revenue Model, R8 Current Status/Go To Market, R9 Communication.

Style notes: warm, curious narrator (Vox cadence, plain words). Cream paper-texture background, one accent color (amber) for the tower, red for risk, green for on track. Every statistic gets a small source tag on screen. Only hyphens as dashes.

## (d) Filming and production notes

Founders shoot (practical):
- Segment 2 on location, golden hour, one lawn-Jenga set (sticky-note labels in marker) and one continuous take plus pickups. Shoot the rewind gag as a real reverse: film the tower falling, then reverse the clip and have a fifth hand with the sticky note enter on top of it.
- Screen recordings of the real product: demo checkout page with the Copilot card sliding in (the key shot), the dashboard, and the AI Advisor chat with the Amazon October question. Record at 4K, tight on the card.
- The trailer's macro thumb-over-Buy-button shot and the kitchen-table tower can also be shot practically with a phone and a real Jenga set.
- Record the narration in a quiet room, one warm voice (a founder or a friend), plus a clean pass of the founders' lines.
- Optional: 2-second student vox-pop clips (phone, vertical OK, crop).

Generated or animated (motion graphics):
- 3D paper-craft tower and the 5,000-futures fan, ring charts, competitor blocks and arrows, market circles, roadmap, logo sting.
- On-screen text, source tags and lower-thirds. Keep each on screen for at least 2 seconds.
- Sound: ka-ching, heartbeat bass, wooden creak, collapse, rewind whoosh, music hit. Light percussive bed under the explainer.

What was cut to fit 3:00, and why:
- Credit score, investments, life-event planner, quarterly PDF reports: left out of the narration; the dashboard beat (2:02 to 2:08) only lists the high-level tiles. They do not earn rubric points beyond the Copilot, Advisor and one-place story.
- Honey/extension trust risk and GLBA and legal-review detail: cut from the narration. The four-field stamp (1:13 to 1:22) carries trust, and "legal review" appears in the ask. Mention GLBA only on the pitch deck or in Q and A.
- Individual founder credentials (clubs, jobs): cut; the HackUTD result and "built in 2 days" are the team credibility.
- TD survey (55% use AI for money, 18% trust it to decide alone): cut; the "code computes, AI writes the sentence" beat covers the same point. Good for the pitch deck.
- Phase II ($25,000): cut. Only the Phase I ask is spoken.
- 315 tests and 92 commits: cut from narration to protect the pace; put them on the pitch deck.

Honesty and guardrails check:
- Every number on screen comes from the team's verified facts. The market numbers are labeled "our estimate". Competitor figures are labeled as Sacra estimates.
- The narration never says the product connects to real bank accounts, never claims it works on Zillow, Best Buy, Apple, CarMax or Amazon checkout, and never calls the output financial advice. The "76 to 89% likely" figure is shown as a demo range, not a promise.
- The name collision is handled: the competitor is always "Copilot Money" in the narration.
